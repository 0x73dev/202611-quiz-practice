Privacy policy

quiz.2026.alias.0x73.dev · Last updated 18 November 2026

This is a small, password-protected study site. This page explains what information the site collects about visitors, why, and what you can do about it.

Who is responsible

The site is run by its owner, who decides what is collected and why. Contact: legal.2026.alias@0x73.dev.

What is collected when you visit

  • Your IP address, and the approximate location (country and city) worked out from it.
  • Your browser, operating system and device type (for example phone or computer).
  • The pages you open, the time and length of your visit, and the link you came from.
  • A recording of how you use the page: clicks, scrolling and what is shown on screen. Anything typed into a text field, including password attempts, is masked and is not recorded.

This happens as soon as the page loads, before you enter the password. The site does not use cookies or store data in your browser for analytics.

Why it is collected

To see whether the site is being used, and to spot unusual or unauthorised access to a password-protected page. The legal basis is the owner's legitimate interest in keeping the site secure. The site is not used for advertising and the data is not sold.

Who handles the data

  • PostHog (EU cloud) stores the visit records and recordings.
  • Cloudflare Web Analytics counts visits in aggregate, without cookies.
  • GitHub Pages hosts the site and may log requests itself under its own privacy statement.

These providers may process data outside the European Economic Area under their own safeguards. See their privacy notices for details.

How long it is kept

Records and recordings are kept only for as long as needed to review activity on the site. The owner reviews them and deletes older data. You can ask for your data to be deleted sooner.

Your rights

You can ask what is held about you, ask for it to be corrected or deleted, or object to the collection. Email legal.2026.alias@0x73.dev and include the approximate time of your visit and your IP address, so the records can be found. You can also complain to the Data Protection Commission in Ireland (dataprotection.ie).

Changes

If this changes, the date at the top is updated.
